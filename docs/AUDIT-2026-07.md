# Cortexel — Deep Audit & Related-Work Report

*Method-level audit of every module, prior-art positioning with citations, ten
critical lenses, and a prioritized "what to fix next" plan. Prepared 2026-07-12
against the working tree of `feat/agentic-nest-visualizations` (not HEAD — the
tree carries substantial uncommitted `core/nest/*` and `react/charts/*` code that
this report treats as part of the system).*

> **Historical, superseded snapshot.** Test counts, sizes, findings, and “shipped”
> statements below describe only that dated worktree. Current status is recorded in
> the README and evidence ledger.

> **How this was produced, and its limits.** Sixteen agents were dispatched to
> read the repo method-by-method; twelve finished (the honesty layer, the skill
> axis, params, the gates, the NEST adapters/analysis/topology, corpus-KG, the
> reference-chart scene, and build/release) before a spend limit halted the run.
> The four un-finished clusters (`VizSpecRenderer`, the 3D knowledge-graph scene,
> the neuron scenes, tests-vs-docs) plus the three highest-severity findings were
> then inspected and **verified directly against source** by hand. The planned
> 12-angle web research and the 3-vote adversarial verification pass were cut by
> the same limit, so prior-art citations below come from one fully-fetched angle
> (declarative viz grammars) plus domain knowledge; treat URLs outside that angle
> as "confirm before publishing." Where I could not verify a claim in code, it is
> marked *(unverified)*. **No audited module surfaced a `critical`-severity gap;
> the total is nine `high`/`medium` items and a long tail of `low` polish** — an
> unusually clean result that itself is a finding.

> **Resolution update (2026-07-13).** Findings 1–3 below are closed in the current
> working tree: single-source/per-series recorder timestamps are strictly increasing,
> typed-array length reads use the intrinsic slot, public diagnostic values are
> bounded/control-safe without object coercion or getter invocation, and negative
> fixtures cover each boundary. The original findings remain documented for audit
> history; roadmap/status prose is updated where it would otherwise imply they are open.
>
> **Resolution update (2026-07-28).** Current evidence must be read per surface.
> Stable `FigureRequestV1` OutputAuthority, artifact, and ledger evidence does not
> certify the legacy `VizSpec`/manifest-v11 contracts, and legacy transforms do not
> certify stable adapters. The current suite includes hand-computable expected-value
> cases plus randomized/property and boundary tests for analysis and topology
> transformations. No pinned external NEST/Elephant differential oracle has run, so
> those repository-local tests are not external scientific certification.
> Legacy raw topology transforms now refuse rank-target-local matrix/degree claims
> without exact target-ownership authority and refuse measured cross-model
> aggregation without bound compatibility/conversion authority. Legacy matrix,
> correlogram, detector, weight-histogram, and phase-plane residual limitations are
> recorded in `KNOWN_LIMITATIONS.md`. Accessibility regressions cover selected
> tokens/marks, caption association, singleton samples, and zero derivatives; they
> are not whole-figure WCAG, grayscale, CVD, host-page, or assistive-technology
> conformance evidence.
>
> **Resolution update (2026-08-01).** The historical “allocation-free `useFrame`”
> conclusion below was too strong. Current guards establish only that
> Cortexel-authored frame callbacks reuse scratch state, do not set React state, and
> avoid a reviewed list of direct allocation syntax. The exactly installed
> `d3-force-3d` 3.0.6 transitively allocates octrees in its many-body and collision
> ticks. Its package peer range is `^3.0.5`, so that source inspection does not transfer
> to a future 3.x resolution. Current presentation/DOM admission is 1,000 nodes and
> 4,000 relationships, while the live force scene is separately capped at 250/1,000.
> The force clock performs at most one tick per rendered frame and at most 60 per
> second; below 60 FPS it settles more slowly. No FPS or frame-time guarantee has been
> established. Auto-framing now performs a provisional seed fit and at most one final
> settled-layout correction, subject to camera authority and user intent.

## 1. Executive summary & verdict

**What Cortexel actually is (shipped).** A TypeScript library that gives an LLM
agent a *typed, hostile-input-hardened, honesty-enforcing path* from spiking-neural
simulation output to a figure. An agent emits a plain-JSON `VizSpec`; a lenient
envelope gate turns "a JS value" into "literal, bounded, acyclic, accessor-free
JSON" (`cloneExactJson`, `core/vizSpec.ts:197-375`); a strict per-skill gate then
checks params against one of 26 closed zod schemas, checks provenance flags, and
computes a **mandatory honesty caption from flags alone**; a checked renderer draws
it — either as a canonical React+SVG chart (19 skills, `react/charts/`) or a
host-injected R3F 3D scene (population voxel hub, neuron cloud, evidence knowledge
graph). Four skills have `scene:null` and route to an *external* renderer
(d3/matplotlib/manim) through a parallel gate. A generated 284 KB
`skills.manifest.json` republishes the whole contract for non-TS hosts. `dist/` is
committed so git-dependency consumers install with no build step. The exact-current
local gate passes 2,186 tests and typecheck. Contract generation is independently
repeated in two zero-state trees and compared byte-for-byte. A local same-worktree
double build also produced identical compiled trees, but there is no retained,
toolchain-bound clean-tree package/tarball reproducibility receipt; the release ledger
therefore correctly leaves that broader claim `NOT_RUN`.

**The verdict.** The engineering is genuinely strong and unusually disciplined for
a pre-1.0 project. Two things are **real, citable novelty**: (1) *fail-closed
scientific-honesty provenance as a security property* — no viz grammar in the
lineage (Vega-Lite, Plotly, ECharts, Gosling, MolViewSpec) derives a tamper-resistant
disclosure from machine-checkable flags and treats a flag that suppresses it as a
bug requiring a test; and (2) *adversarial hardening of the spec envelope* — treating
the spec author as hostile (reject `__proto__`, `-0`, sparse arrays, `JSON.rawJSON`,
Proxy length-traps, accessors) is exactly right when the author is an LLM, and
nobody else in this space does it. **The overstated claim** is "grammar": Cortexel
has no encoding channels, no transforms, no composition algebra, no interaction — the
four pillars that define a grammar of graphics. It is a *closed catalog of
parameterized scene templates*, an architectural sibling of Plotly's trace schema,
not of Vega-Lite. That is a legitimate and arguably *safer* design for an LLM target
(a bounded enum is easier to validate, route, and repair than an open algebra), but
the README should say "hardened, honesty-enforcing figure catalog for agents," not
"grammar."

**The central premise — does it hold?** Partly, and the gap is the most important
finding in this report. Cortexel makes figures *provably well-formed and provably
labelled*, and it makes a specific class of dishonest **rendering** impossible
(interpolating across gaps, painting structural absence as measured zero, bridging
omitted bins). It does **not** make figures *true*: every provenance flag is
self-asserted by the same agent that made the figure, and nothing checks that the
rendered **data** matches the claimed provenance
(`core/skills/provenanceKeys.ts:289-328` verifies declared-string == param-string
equality, nothing more). Honesty is enforced on *form*, asserted on *substance*.
That is a defensible scope — but the docs oversell it, and there is at least one
input shape where the form-honesty itself **fails open** (see the headline table).

**Original verification status.** Findings 1–3 reproduced when this audit was first
written and have since been closed as recorded above. Finding 5 arose from the
schema-shape comparison but its Float32-time recommendation was refuted during
repair review: millisecond time axes deliberately retain binary64 precision and do
not feed Float32 value buffers.

**One audit concern was refuted on inspection.** The `contract-core` audit worried
that `VizSpecRenderer`'s default path uses the *lenient* `validateVizSpec`. It does
not: the renderer uses the strict `validateSkillInvocation` whenever a skill id is
known, and even skill-less specs use strict `validateSpec` unless the caller
explicitly opts into `trustedEnvelope` (`react/VizSpecRenderer.tsx:145-160`). The
lenient path is reachable only via an explicit trust opt-in. Corrected here so it
does not propagate as a false problem.

## 2. What is included — a complete inventory

### 2.1 The contract core (`core/vizSpec.ts`, `core/designLaws.ts`, barrels)
- **`SCENE_NAMES`** (frozen tuple) drives both the `SceneName` TS union and the
  runtime zod enum — a genuine single source of truth (`designLaws.ts:6`,
  `vizSpec.ts:445`).
- **`cloneExactJson`** (`vizSpec.ts:197-375`, ~178 LOC) is the trust boundary: it
  rejects non-finite numbers, `-0`, cycles, sparse/decorated arrays, accessors,
  symbols, non-plain prototypes, `BigInt`, `undefined`, functions, and
  `toJSON`/`rawJSON` shape games, within depth/node/string budgets
  (`CORTEXEL_JSON_LIMITS`: depth 32, 500k nodes, 100k string, 5M total).
- **`ProvenanceSchema`** pins `calibrated_posterior: z.literal(false)`
  (`vizSpec.ts:406`) — the load-bearing honesty invariant.
- Frozen policy constants (`CORTEXEL_JSON_POLICY`, `ENVELOPE_NORMALIZATION_POLICY`)
  are mirrored into the manifest for non-TS hosts.
- **Caveat:** the envelope is *lenient by design* — it does not check that `skill`
  is a real `SKILL_ID` or that `skill`↔`scene` agree (`vizSpec.ts:452`). Strictness
  lives downstream in the skill gate. `designLaws.ts` also leaks ~8 dead
  backdrop-era exports (`SCENE_FRAMING`, `CAMERA_PRESETS`, `NeuralScene*`, …) into
  the public API via `export *`.

### 2.2 The skill axis (`skillIds.ts`, `registry.ts` 2657 LOC, `router.ts`)
- 26 ids in a frozen tuple → a `Record`-typed `SKILL_REGISTRY` (key completeness is
  type-enforced). Each contract carries device family, scene-or-null, required
  params/provenance flags/keys, portable cross-field constraint declarations, and a
  worked example.
- **`router.ts`** is the executable `nest.viz_router`: it maps a raw NEST dict to a
  concrete skill+scene, fail-closed for unknown/ambiguous/`scene:null`. The
  family→skill index is *derived* from the registry at load with a duplicate-kind
  throw (`router` derivation, not hand-written).
- **Structural risk:** the registry is 2657 hand-written lines; the four matrix
  skills and two degree skills are near-verbatim copy-paste
  (`registry.ts:914-1131`). Constraint-kind names are triplicated (union type +
  kinds array + semantics object) with only ~10 of 35 spot-checked
  (`registry.ts:98-133,236-272,273-451`). `describeSkill` hand-clones the same
  nested shape the freeze loop hand-enumerates — two parallel field lists that must
  be edited in lockstep.

### 2.3 Params schemas (`params.ts` 2299 LOC)
One strict `.strict()` zod schema per skill + shared primitives (`gpuNumber`
Float32-clamped, `gpuArray`, `idArray`, `timeArray`, `displayText`) + reusable
`superRefine` helpers (monotonicity, uniform bins, normalized-mass, sparse-matrix,
window-edge, spatial-bounds). These bounds *are* the published contract (re-emitted
into the manifest). `timeArray` intentionally retains binary64 precision and is
not a Float32 value-buffer input; treating that difference as a defect was the
refuted finding 5. Other historical inconsistencies included three different
`bin_width_ms` bounds across sibling skills, `Object.is` (bit-exact) derived-value
tolerance for delay versus `approximatelyEqual` for degree, and the
`Spatial3DObjectSchema` / `animation_replay` escape hatches from “typos fail
closed.”

### 2.4 The gates (`paramPreflight.ts` 867, `validateSkillInvocation.ts` 616)
Two-stage preflight (raw pre-clone length fuse → post-clone element fuse) *before*
the zod schema, which is the correct amplification guard (`preflight before parse`).
`calibrated_posterior===true` and version mismatch are read from the raw payload
before `validateVizSpec`. The gate returns the mandatory caption so the renderer
cannot omit it. **Weaknesses:** per-skill field shapes are duplicated across four
sites (`ALLOWED_PARAM_FIELDS` + two preflight switches + registry zod) with no single
source; the raw pre-clone fuse is TOCTOU-defeatable by a Proxy (validation stays
correct, only amplification-avoidance fails); and the three bidi/`toString` leaks
above.

### 2.5 Honesty layer (`provenance.ts`, `safeRuntime.ts`, `provenanceKeys.ts`)
`mandatoryDisclosure(p)` computes a prefix from flags; a caller `caption` is
appended only as a re-escaped, bidi-isolated **"Caller note (unverified)."**
`provenanceParamConstraintError` checks declared↔param *string equality* under
`Object.is` with prototype-safe path walking. **Two honesty caveats verified:** the
header claims the disclosure derives "only from machine-checkable flags," but
`mandatoryDisclosure` also branches on `p.source` (caller free text,
`provenance.ts:90-91`); and nothing verifies the DATA is real — only that declared
strings match param strings.

### 2.6 Host-invocation escape hatch (`hostInvocation.ts`, `verify.ts`, `authoring.ts`, `examples.ts`)
`scene:null` skills route to an external renderer through
`validateHostRendererInvocation`, which enforces the *same* params/provenance/version
rigor as the in-process gate (it is **not** a validation bypass) but only *returns*
the caption. `detectEmptyScene` (verify.ts, one ~290-LOC function) re-validates
SceneData shape against a hand-maintained `SCENE_DATA_FIELDS` mirror. `authoring.ts`
is the author→validate→repair loop with prompt-injection-safe error formatting.

### 2.7 NEST boundary (`adapters.ts`, `shapes.ts`, `safeInput.ts`, `analysis.ts`, `topology.ts`)
- **`safeInput.snapshotNestInput`** is the hostile-input wall (500k-node cap,
  prototype/accessor/cycle/depth checks) before zod re-hardens with descriptor
  snapshots. Accepts numeric typed arrays (unlike the strict VizSpec boundary).
- **`analysis.ts`** (677 LOC): population rate, ISI, PSTH, correlogram — half-open
  `[start, stop)` binning with a bounded roundoff snap capped at 1e-7 fractional,
  failing closed to `INDETERMINATE` beyond it. Population rate normalized by
  *declared* sender count (correct). ISI computed within-sender only. The legacy
  correlogram envelope checks its internal lag/statistic geometry. Its raw
  transform now requires literal port order, simulation resolution/bounds, an
  exact odd resolution multiple, and both edge-window margins. Those are
  caller-supplied configuration rather than independent source authentication;
  serialized params retain no configuration/transform receipt, and a hand-authored
  `excluded_self_pairs` claim remains external. The official
  [`correlation_detector` reference](https://nest-simulator.readthedocs.io/en/v3.10/models/correlation_detector.html)
  documents the port selection and resolution/window conditions that an external
  authority must preserve.
- **`topology.ts`** (1127 LOC): connection graph, adjacency/weight/delay matrices,
  degree/delay distributions, spatial map. Mandatory declared node universe so
  isolates survive; singular/plural SynapseCollection forms accepted but never
  mixed, scalars never broadcast; multapses preserved; structural absence never
  painted as measured zero. The current raw boundary refuses
  `mpi_target_rank_local` for all three matrices and both degree transforms, and
  the strict params gates enforce the same restriction, because exact rank-owned
  target authority is not bound; NEST documents that
  [`GetConnections()` returns only target-local connections on the executing MPI process](https://nest-simulator.readthedocs.io/en/v3.10/ref_material/pynest_api/nest.lib.hl_api_connections.html).
  Nonempty measured transforms also refuse weight/delay aggregation over multiple
  observed synapse models without an explicit compatibility/conversion authority.
  Hand-authored matrix aggregates remain structurally checked rather than
  independently rederived from raw measurements. The raw weight-histogram
  transform now derives one bounded observation per selected connection from a
  weight/model channel complete for the declared scope, but standalone serialized
  aggregate params carry no raw-entry receipt, so that provenance remains
  externally disclosed.
- **Boundary hardening follow-up — `resolved`:** shared single-source/per-series
  timestamps reject duplicates, typed-array lengths use the intrinsic slot rather
  than subclass accessors, and recorder adapters share the deep safe-input snapshot.

### 2.8 Corpus knowledge graph (`corpusKnowledgeGraph.ts` + `KnowledgeGraph3DParamsSchema`)
A no-throw adapter from an Engram `CorpusEntityGraphResponse` to strict params:
epistemics are a frozen `DERIVED_ADVISORY` spread onto every element (never read
from input), and the output is re-validated through the strict schema. The
~150-LOC `superRefine` enforces duplicate-id rejection, endpoint-kind matching,
≤9 parallel edges/pair, and "evidence cannot terminate inside the graph."
**Follow-up — fabrication resolved, resolution still limited:** the adapter no longer
emits self-derived `graph_snapshot_record` anchors. Every upstream node and assertion
must provide its own typed evidence array, which is retained and revalidated.
The legacy params surface still has no top-level evidence-record inventory, however,
so a supplied record/citation/source identifier is structurally checked but not
resolved or authenticated.

### 2.9 Render layer
- **`VizSpecRenderer`** (strict by default; verified §1) memoizes a detached
  validated snapshot by spec identity and bidi-isolates the caption in `<bdi>`.
- **`ReferenceChartScene`** (1834 LOC / 70 KB): 15 chart functions + `ChartShell` +
  paginated DOM mirror + dispatcher. Honesty is expressed as `data-*` count
  disclosures wherever compaction occurs; distributions compact only by
  mass-preserving adjacent aggregation; matrices distinguish absent-background from
  present-zero. **Gaps:** non-compacting charts (traces, raster, histograms) have
  *no* render budget (a size-based DoM DoS at 50k samples), and only 5 of 15 charts
  expose a paginated `dataRows` DOM mirror.
- **Legacy phase-plane/accessibility follow-up:** the current phase-plane gate
  requires two distinct axes with at least two strictly increasing finite
  coordinates, exact grid/derivative/unit key parity, full Cartesian derivative
  cardinality, and derivative units structurally bound to the corresponding axis
  units over one common explicit time denominator. Per-second components use one
  binary64 division by 1000 and underflowing nonzero values fail closed; this is
  not a universal byte-identity claim across independently rounded source units.
  That syntax does not
  authenticate the model computation or unit claims. Zero derivatives are now
  visible rings explicitly disclosed as samples rather than equilibria, singleton
  plasticity has a visible mark, and the legacy honesty caption is associated to
  its figure group. These are focused regressions, not whole-page, WCAG, grayscale,
  CVD, browser, or assistive-technology certification.
- **`KnowledgeGraph3DScene`** (873 LOC in this historical snapshot): d3-force-3d
  layout, instanced meshes, `frustumCulled={false}` on streamed geometry, camera writes
  opt-in, local `CanvasTexture` labels (no CDN fonts). The direct first-party callback
  reused module-scope scratch and avoided reviewed allocation syntax; that source-level
  observation did not establish allocation-free execution. Current inspection of the
  exact installed d3-force-3d 3.0.6 shows transitive octree allocation during force
  ticks.
- **Neuron scenes + shaders:** `MeshBasic` for passive data, emissive capped ≈1.15
  luminance for active events (design law 2 holds, `neuronShaders.ts:85-89`).
- A11y companions (`KnowledgeGraphA11yList`, `PopulationA11yList`, `NeuronA11yPager`)
  paginate the WebGL content into the DOM, because meshes never enter the browser
  a11y tree.

### 2.10 Build/release
Committed `dist/` (ESM+CJS+`.d.ts`+`.d.cts` × 5 subpaths) gated by
`git status --porcelain -- dist` after a fresh build; esbuild pinned via `overrides`
+ frozen lockfile for reproducibility (verified deterministic). `emit-manifest.ts`
generates the 284 KB manifest from *source*. `smoke-package.ts` installs the real
tarball across Node 20/22/24 and probes every subpath's ESM/CJS/types. **Four
decoupled version axes** (package 0.5.0 / manifest '8' / spec 1.3.0 / skill-axis
1.6.0) with no unifying guard.

## 3. Related work & prior art

*Citation verification update (2026-08-01): links in §§3.1–3.8 were checked
against publisher records, standards bodies, project documentation, or
first-party repositories. A link establishes the cited work's identity and stated
scope; it does not establish that Cortexel interoperates with it or inherits its
empirical evidence.*

### 3.1 Declarative viz grammars (Cortexel's true lineage)
- **[Vega-Lite: A Grammar of Interactive Graphics](https://vis.csail.mit.edu/pubs/vega-lite/)**
  (Satyanarayan et al., IEEE TVCG/InfoVis 2017) — the yardstick. Cortexel is *not* a
  grammar in this sense: no encoding channels, transforms, composition, or
  interaction. Cite it to *contrast*, not to claim kinship.
- **[Vega/Vega-Lite JSON Schema](https://github.com/vega/schema)** — versioning is
  done via a resolvable `$schema` URL with major/minor resolution. Cortexel has a
  single exact-literal `specVersion` and no bindable schema URL. Adopt this.
- **[Plotly figure schema](https://plotly.com/python/figure-structure/)** — this,
  not Vega-Lite, is Cortexel's architectural sibling: a closed enum of parameterized
  figure types with strict per-field validation. Frame Cortexel relative to Plotly.
- **[MolViewSpec](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12230705/)** (Mol*
  extension, 2024/25) — the closest existing analog to Cortexel's exact design
  (declarative 3D scientific scene, host-agnostic, multi-renderer, JSON-Schema +
  **Pydantic** dual validation that actually ships). Cortexel's "host SHOULD mirror
  as a Pydantic model" is aspirational; MolViewSpec ships it. **Prior art for the
  pattern — must cite.**
- **[Gosling](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/9557192/)** (TVCG 2022) — a
  domain-specific scientific grammar that stayed *compositional* while specializing
  to genomics. The right precedent for "should Cortexel have a track/mark layer
  instead of a fixed enum?"
- **[A Declarative Grammar of Flexible Volume Visualization Pipelines](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30130223/)**
  (TVCG/SciVis 2018) — a declarative scivis-3D vocabulary Cortexel could borrow
  instead of hardcoding one R3F scene per skill.
- **[Mosaic vgplot](https://idl.uw.edu/mosaic/vgplot/)** (VIS 2024) and
  **[Nebula](https://shuxinhuan.github.io/pub/nebula.pdf)** (TVCG 2021) — mark the
  two dimensions Cortexel omits: data-source/lazy-query binding (Cortexel inlines
  bounded literal data, 500k-node ceiling) and multi-panel view coordination.

### 3.2 LLM chart generation & spec-repair loops
- **[VegaChat](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.15385)** (2026 preprint) studies LLM
  generation, validation, and assessment of declarative charts. It is relevant to
  Cortexel's generate → validate loop; Cortexel's typed repair codes and honesty
  gate are separate design choices, not evidence inherited from VegaChat.
- **[Visualization Generation with LLMs: An Evaluation](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.11255)**
  (arXiv 2024) — evidence that agents frequently emit invalid/subtly-wrong specs;
  the empirical justification for closed-enum + fail-closed design.
- **[LIDA](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/project/lida-automatic-generation-of-grammar-agnostic-visualizations/)**
  (Microsoft Research),
  **[Data Formulator](https://github.com/microsoft/data-formulator)**,
  **[Draco / Draco 2](https://dig.cmu.edu/draco2/)**,
  **[Chat2VIS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.02094)**, and
  **[nvBench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12926)** are adjacent systems and
  benchmarks. Draco is the important comparator: its published system represents
  visualization-design knowledge as hard and soft constraints solved with
  Clingo/answer-set programming. Cortexel's per-skill `superRefine` checks are an
  architectural analogy only; they are imperative checks, not a declarative
  solver or a scientific-equivalence result.

### 3.3 Honesty, deception & mirages in visualization
- **[McNutt, Kindlmann & Correll, "Surfacing Visualization
  Mirages"](https://doi.org/10.1145/3313831.3376420)** (CHI 2020) provides a
  conceptual model and metamorphic-testing method for figures that can mislead
  despite being structurally valid. Cortexel's no-interpolation-across-gaps,
  no-absence-as-zero, and mass-preserving-compaction rules address narrower,
  explicitly encoded failure modes; the paper does not validate those rules.
- Prior work spans solver-backed validation in
  **[Draco](https://dig.cmu.edu/draco2/)**, in-situ chart-error annotation in
  **[VisuaLint](https://vis.csail.mit.edu/pubs/visualint/)**, and an empirical
  study of common distortions by
  **[Pandey et al.](https://doi.org/10.1145/2702123.2702608)**. Cortexel resembles
  a fail-closed pre-render contract more than these authoring or post-hoc
  assistants; that is an architectural comparison, not measured superiority.
- **[Hypothetical outcome plots](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0142444)**
  exemplify an uncertainty-visualization family the audited snapshot does not
  engage. Its contract exposes no first-class error-bar, confidence-interval,
  ensemble, or HOP capability despite Cortexel's epistemic-honesty positioning.
  This remains a scientific-credibility gap after external differential
  validation.

### 3.4 Provenance standards
- Relevant prior art includes
  **[W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/)** (a W3C Recommendation), the
  separately submitted **[PROV-JSON](https://www.w3.org/Submission/prov-json/)**
  serialization (a W3C Member Submission),
  **[RO-Crate](https://www.researchobject.org/ro-crate/specification.html)**,
  **[nanopublication community guidelines](https://nanopub.net/docs/)**,
  **[MLCommons Croissant](https://docs.mlcommons.org/croissant/docs/croissant-spec-1.1.html)**,
  and **[VisTrails](https://doi.org/10.1109/MCSE.2007.106)**. These artifacts do
  not all have the same standards status or scope. The audited snapshot's
  provenance object is bespoke and *thin* (a handful of self-asserted booleans and
  a source string), and it does not interoperate with them. This is a positioning
  weakness: a reviewer can reasonably ask why Cortexel does not emit PROV or embed
  an RO-Crate. The honest answer is that Cortexel's provenance is a *disclosure
  trigger*, not a *derivation record*; it should cite PROV as a deliberate non-goal
  and consider a PROV/RO-Crate sidecar for standard archival tooling.

## 4. Architecture & design-choice critique

**Closed 26-skill enum vs a compositional grammar — `defensible`, and correct for
the threat model.** The LLM-eval literature (§3.2) shows agents struggle with open
composition algebras; a bounded enum is far easier to validate, route, and repair.
The cost is real — when an agent needs a figure not in the enum, it has *no path*
except the `scene:null` host escape hatch — but for a safety-first agent target this
is the right trade. **Recommendation:** keep the enum; add one Gosling-style
compositional "custom panel" skill later if demand appears, not now.

**Hand-written 2657-line registry + 2299-line params — `questionable`.** The four
matrix skills and two degree skills are copy-paste (`registry.ts:914-1131`); the
constraint-kind names are triplicated with partial test coverage; `describeSkill`
hand-clones what the freeze loop hand-enumerates. The repo's own non-negotiable is
"single sources of truth — don't hand-edit derived data," and this is the largest
violation of its own rule. **Alternative:** a small factory that generates the
matrix/degree families from shared descriptors (cuts ~400 LOC and the drift risk),
and derive the constraint-kinds array + a "every used kind has semantics"
exhaustiveness test from the semantics object keys. This is the highest-leverage
maintainability fix.

**zod-as-contract + hand-authored JSON-Schema manifest — `defensible` but
un-parity-tested.** `JSON_PARAMS_PORTABLE_SCHEMA` is `additionalProperties:true` and
expresses *none* of the runtime exact-JSON guarantees, so a non-TS host validating
only against the manifest gets a materially weaker gate than the TS runtime
(`vizSpec.ts:94-104`). No test asserts the hand-authored portable schema stays in
parity with the zod shape. **Fix:** either generate the portable schema from zod, or
add a parity test; and document that the manifest is a *description*, not an
*equivalent gate*.

**Exact-JSON gate + amplification-safe preflight ordering — `sound`.** Preflighting
before the heavy parse is the correct DoS guard, and it is done in both entrypoints.
The one caveat (Proxy TOCTOU on the raw pre-clone fuse) does not affect
*correctness*, only amplification-avoidance, and the 500k global cap still bounds it.

**Fail-closed honesty + self-asserted flags — `sound design, oversold claim`.** The
enforcement (literal-false pin, append-only caller note, bidi isolation) is
genuinely good. But (a) it is 100% load-bearing on one literal (finding 4), (b) the
"flags only, never caller text" claim is false because `mandatoryDisclosure` sniffs
`p.source` (finding, §2.5), and (c) it verifies *labels*, not *data*. **Fix the
docs first** (they currently overclaim), then consider gating the caption on a
positive evidence token rather than the absence of four negatives.

**`scene:null` as convention not enforcement — `defensible` (unavoidable for a pure
library).** A validation library *cannot* force an external matplotlib process to
render a caption. The honest framing is "Cortexel guarantees the caption is computed
and handed over; the host is contractually obligated to show it." That is fine — but
SECURITY.md's "must" should be softened to "the library cannot enforce this; hosts
that drop the caption are non-conformant," and a conformance test-kit for hosts
would give the obligation teeth.

**Committed `dist/` — `sound`.** Verified deterministic; the drift gate is a real
guard; it enables zero-build git installs. The cost (noisy diffs, every PR must
rebuild) is accepted knowingly. Keep it.

**The five design laws — `law for 3, source discipline for 4, aesthetics for 1 & 2`.**
Law 3 (honesty fails closed) has semantic executable guards. Law 4's executable guard
is lexical: it checks direct Cortexel-authored frame syntax, scratch reuse, and absence
of React state updates, not heap behavior in callees. It therefore cannot establish
allocation-free execution; exact d3-force-3d 3.0.6 force ticks allocate octrees.
Laws 1 & 2 (sphere/voxel grammar, emissive ≤1.15) are *aesthetic conventions* dressed
as laws; law 2 has a guard but law 1 is unfalsifiable. Fine to keep, but call them
"conventions" where they are conventions.

**The 70 KB `ReferenceChartScene` — `defensible, at its ceiling`.** All pure geometry
is correctly factored into `chartGeometry.ts`/`topologyGeometry.ts`; the file is big
because 15 charts share a `ChartShell`. The maintenance smell is the ~30-line shell
prop block repeated 15× and the absence of any `useMemo` (every chart rebuilds
domains/Sets/Maps per render). **Fix:** one file per skill under `charts/skills/`
with a generated dispatcher; memoize geometry by params identity.

**Version triple/quadruple — `questionable`.** Four independent version axes with no
unifying guard means no single number tells a reviewer whether the contract changed,
and the manifest version '8' is a hand-typed literal that the smoke test also pins to
'8' — so a manifest content change with a forgotten bump passes every gate. **Fix:**
derive `manifestVersion` from a content hash, and document the version matrix in one
place.

**Standalone-source governance — `resolved`.** This repository is the canonical,
writable Cortexel source. Engram is a downstream consumer that pins published commits
and carries generated contract snapshots; it must not recreate an in-tree Cortexel
source copy. CONTRIBUTING and the agent guide now state the same workflow.

## 6. The ten lenses

**1. Contract & agent DX — `strong loop, closed-enum ceiling`.** The
discover→author→validate→repair loop is genuinely closable by a model with no human:
structured repair carries hint + example + `didYouMean`, and it is prompt-injection
safe. The ceiling is the closed enum — when the agent needs a figure not in the 26,
its only path is the `scene:null` host hatch. *Finding:* routing to a "weak" skill
drops the `weakDisclosure` signal on the success path (`router.ts:61-62`), so an
agent isn't told a derived-view disclosure is mandatory without a second call.

**2. Security & adversarial robustness — `best-in-class for the class, three real
leaks`.** The envelope hardening is the strongest part of the codebase and is novel
for a viz spec. But honesty *fails open* on finding 1, three paths leak hostile text
into agent-facing output (findings 2/3), and a TypedArray-subclass getter leak
breaches the no-accessor invariant. None are critical; all are fixable in a day.

**3. Scientific correctness & epistemics — `honest form, asserted substance`.**
Repository-local checks support the half-open, bounded-snap, fail-closed binning
rules. But provenance flags are self-asserted, nothing checks data-vs-claim, there
is **no uncertainty
representation**, and the analyses have repository-local numeric/property evidence
but no pinned external differential validation against Elephant/NEST. The
disclosure caption is the only defense against a confidently-flagged fabrication —
and it leans entirely on one literal pin (finding 4).

**4. Prior art, novelty & positioning — `two real contributions, one wrong word`.**
Novel and citable: fail-closed in-spec honesty; hostile-input hardening. Reinvention:
the repair loop (VegaChat/LIDA), the provenance object (worse than PROV), the
analyses (Elephant). Wrong word: "grammar." See §3.9.

**5. Renderer & graphics — `sound, with bounded but unprofiled live-force work`.**
Instancing, culling, bloom-budget, and CDN-free labels are handled correctly. Current
first-party frame callbacks reuse scratch and avoid React state updates, but exact
d3-force-3d 3.0.6 ticks allocate octrees; the lexical guard proves no stronger claim.
The canonical graph admits 1,000 nodes/4,000 relationships for presentation and DOM
inspection but mounts the live force scene only at 250/1,000 or below. Above that live
cap it retains caption, legend, operable DOM, and the full paginated record browser; an
exact source-bound under-cap filter can regain the visual. At most one force tick runs
per rendered frame (at most 60 per second), so low frame rates settle more slowly and
there is no FPS guarantee. Other gaps remain: non-compacting charts have no budget (DOM
DoS), no `useMemo` anywhere in the chart scene, and headless export is absent (roadmap).

**6. Accessibility & perception — `right pattern, partial coverage`.** DOM companions
for WebGL and paginated mirrors match Data Navigator's philosophy; `crameri` default
is good. Selected token/mark contrast and non-colour tuple structure now have
regressions, as do the legacy caption association, singleton sample, and zero-vector
marks. Only 5/15 historical SVG charts expose values, however, and no whole-figure
WCAG, grayscale, CVD, host-page, browser, or AT study has run; the emissive-bloom
neuron style also has no certified low-vision alternative.

**7. Interoperability & ecosystem fit — `the existential question`.** Cortexel is TS
in a Python field. NEST users live in PyNEST+Elephant+Viziphant+Neo+matplotlib. The
language-neutral manifest is a real bridge *only if a Python validator ships* (it
doesn't yet — it's aspirational, unlike MolViewSpec). Ranked unlocks: Python/Pydantic
validator > Elephant/Neo adapters > MCP server > headless export > Jupyter/anywidget.

**8. Architecture & maintainability — `disciplined, but violates its own single-source
rule`.** The axis wiring (SCENE_NAMES, SKILL_IDS→registry, derived router) is
exemplary. The exceptions are large: 2657-line hand-written registry with copy-paste
families, triplicated constraint kinds, hand-mirrored clone/freeze loops, and
hand-authored portable schemas. Ajv example/negative-shape checks and portable
constraint-evaluator coverage reduce drift risk but are not an exhaustive
cross-language equivalence proof.

**9. Testing, verification & release — `broad repository-local coverage, no external
differential receipt`.** The historical 441 tests included executable invariant
guards (honesty, design laws, manifest parity). Current tests additionally include
hand-computable values, randomized/property cases, and numerical boundaries for
transformations. They still do not substitute for a pinned NEST/Elephant
differential corpus. The historical version-quadruple and
`react-test-renderer` concerns should be re-evaluated against the current tree.

**10. Product, adoption & governance — `strong artifact, invisible product`.** The
positioning is over-worded ("grammar"), there is no live gallery or playground (you
cannot see a figure without installing), and there is no published validation study
against real NEST data. Governance no longer blocks external code PRs: this standalone
repository is the canonical writable source, while Engram consumes pinned commits and
generated contract snapshots. The remaining sequenced steps in §5 are the path from
"impressive repo" to "tool neuroscientists adopt."

## 7. Blind spots & limits of this analysis

1. **The original web-research phase was cut; this citation pass is later and
   bounded.** On 2026-08-01, the named works in §§3.2–3.8 were checked against
   publisher records, standards bodies, project documentation, or first-party
   repositories. That verifies identity and stated scope and corrects the
   Crameri/Borland attribution; it is not a systematic literature review,
   reproduction, or endorsement. VegaChat 2601.15385 now resolves as a January
   2026 preprint.
2. **The 3-vote adversarial verification pass never ran.** I hand-verified findings
   1–5 against source; findings 6–7 and the `low`-severity tail rest on the audit
   agents' reads, not independent refutation. Treat `low` items as leads.
3. **Four modules were audited by me directly, not by the deep agents**
   (`VizSpecRenderer`, the 3D KG scene, the neuron scenes, tests-vs-docs). My passes
   were targeted spot-checks (strict-path, direct `useFrame` syntax/scratch reuse,
   emissive clamp,
   fuzz-test absence), not exhaustive method-by-method reads. A full audit of the KG
   scene's picking/raycasting and the neuron shaders' numerical behavior is still
   owed.
4. **No pinned external differential run was executed.** This historical audit did
   not numerically compare Cortexel with Elephant/NEST. The current suite does check
   hand-computable expected values and randomized/property/boundary cases, so the
   former “nobody checked the numbers” statement is stale. Finding 7 remains open
   only in its narrowed form: repository-local tests cannot establish equivalence
   to a version-pinned external implementation or real simulator output.
5. **Performance is unmeasured.** "70 KB file," "no `useMemo`," "O(n log n) allocation
   in the comparator" are structural observations. The current knowledge-graph force
   scene refuses views above 250 nodes/1,000 relationships, but no FPS or frame-time
   profile at that admitted boundary has run; presentation/DOM inspection separately
   admits 1,000/4,000. Performance claims for other large visualization surfaces remain
   untested.
6. **The manifest was not validated as a contract for a real non-TS consumer.** I
   confirmed it is generated and 284 KB; I did not stand up a Python/Rust host and
   check that validating against it yields the same accept/reject decisions as the TS
   runtime. §3.9 asserts the portable schema is weaker *from reading it*, not from
   running it.
7. **Docs-vs-code parity is only sampled.** The `tests-docs` audit cluster was one of
   the four killed by the spend limit, so the systematic "every README claim vs the
   code" pass is incomplete; §1 and §4 caught the "grammar" and "flags-only" overclaims
   opportunistically, but a full sweep is owed.
